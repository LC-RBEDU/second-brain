# Writing taste

## Core voice

Aim for: concise, human, grounded, slightly bold, commercially clear, non-generic.

Avoid: polished-but-empty consulting language, AI hype, literal corporate-English translations into Czech, repetitive abstractions, and motivational cliches.

## Claims and headlines

Preferred pattern:

- Usually 2-4 words for the main title or claim.
- Strong tension, image, or point of view beats a descriptive course label.
- A short metaphorical title can be followed by a clarifying subtitle.
- Keep title and claim distinct; do not make both explain the same thing.
- English is fine when it genuinely sounds better. Do not force English for prestige.

Calibrated positive examples from prior work:

- "Bod zlomu"
- "Druhý mozek"
- "Pod tlakem"
- "Beyond the First Answer"
- "Challenge Your Thinking"
- "První odpověď nestačí"
- "AI tam, kde dává smysl"
- "Směr ve změně"
- "Změna bez přetížení"
- "Build the vision. Align priorities. Learn AI by doing."

Use these as taste calibration, not as phrases to recycle indiscriminately.

## Anti-patterns

Usually reject or rewrite patterns like:

- "Empowering Leaders for the Future"
- "Unlock Your Leadership Potential"
- "Transforming Leadership for Tomorrow"
- generic "future-ready", "journey to excellence", "unlocking potential", "navigating the future" language unless the brief genuinely calls for it
- long titles that explain the entire workshop
- headings that merely repeat the paragraph below them
- labels like "workshop", "lab", or "simulation" as the creative title when a stronger concept is available

## Czech style

- Prefer natural Czech over translated English syntax.
- Prefer verbs and concrete nouns over stacked abstract nouns.
- Avoid inflated phrasing such as "rozvoj kompetencí vedoucích pracovníků v kontextu transformační změny" when "Jak vést lidi změnou" communicates the point.
- Keep bullets short enough to scan on a slide.
- Do not alternate between direct "ty" and impersonal "účastníci" within one artifact unless the client-facing voice requires it.

## English style

Prefer crisp business English with an idea behind it. Avoid generic conference language. Short parallel phrasing can work when it is concrete, e.g. "Build the vision. Align priorities. Learn AI by doing."

## Humanization test

Before finalizing, remove at least one unnecessary abstraction from any sentence that contains three or more abstract nouns. If a sentence sounds like it came from a proposal template, rewrite it around the client's situation or a visible action/result.
