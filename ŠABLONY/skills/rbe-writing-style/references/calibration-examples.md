# Calibration examples

Use examples to infer taste, not as fixed templates.

## Example 1 - generic claim -> sharper claim

Weak:
"Empowering Leaders for the Future"

Why it misses:
Could belong to any consultancy. No tension, no point of view, no client-specific idea.

Closer to the preferred taste:
"Beyond the First Answer"

Why it works better:
Short, has tension, opens a story, and does not explain the whole workshop.

## Example 2 - translated/corporate Czech -> natural Czech

Weak:
"Rozvoj schopnosti navigovat zmenou v prostredi zvysene komplexity"

Better:
"Smer ve zmene"

Use the body copy to explain the scope. Do not make the title carry the whole brief.

## Example 3 - activity list -> value

Weak "Co si kupujete":
- 4h workshop
- facilitator
- materials

Better:
- tailored work on the team's live case
- a communication storyline the team can actually use
- practice with difficult reactions and feedback

The price should map to what becomes possible or tangible.

## Example 4 - duplicate slides

Bad architecture:
Slide 3: What the workshop covers
Slide 4: Workshop agenda with the same three topics

Better architecture:
Slide 3: What changes for participants / learning intent
Slide 4: How the experience works / practice, simulation, outputs

Different slide = different question.

## Example 5 - scope variants

Weak:
Half-day: shorter version of the full-day workshop.
Full-day: full version of the workshop.

Better:
Half-day: focus on one live case, core communication structure, one round of practice.
Full-day: build the storyline, map stakeholders/reactions, repeated practice, refine the message, leave with a ready-to-use output.

Make trade-offs visible and credible.

## Example 6 - independent redesign

When a colleague's draft or transcript is provided, do not preserve its structure by default. First identify:
- the actual business need
- the participant behavior that must change
- the live case or decision
- the minimum viable learning experience

Then choose the format. The user values independent design judgment.

## Example 7 - naming

Approved/liked naming signals have included:
- "Bod zlomu"
- "Druhy mozek"
- "Pod tlakem"
- "AI tam, kde dava smysl"
- "Prvni odpoved nestaci"

This suggests a preference for short phrases that contain tension, image, or an everyday truth. Avoid repeatedly reusing the same metaphor family.

## Example 8 - integrated AI learning

Preferred direction:
"Build the vision. Align priorities. Learn AI by doing."

Design implication:
Teach AI through the work participants already need to do. Avoid a detached "AI theory" block unless the audience needs a basic common foundation first.
